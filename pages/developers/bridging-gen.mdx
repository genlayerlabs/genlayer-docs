# Bridging GEN (L1 ↔ L2)

GenLayer Chain is an Arbitrum Orbit L2. A deposit moves ERC-20 GEN on Ethereum to native GEN on GenLayer Chain. A withdrawal returns native GEN to Ethereum as ERC-20 GEN. Bradbury Phase 2 settles to Ethereum Sepolia and uses AnyTrust for data availability.

This guide follows a transfer through the canonical bridge from the first wallet signature to the balance change on the destination chain.

<Callout type="warning">
  For Bradbury Phase 2, open the [GenLayer testnet bridge on Rollbridge](https://genlayer-chain-testnet-cfn8m3xc6l-86e44e69a1216817.testnets.rollbridge.app/). Check that the route connects Ethereum Sepolia (11155111) and GenLayer Chain (4221). Verify the [Phase 2 genesis hash](/developers/networks#testnet-bradbury) and the [Sepolia GEN token](https://sepolia.etherscan.io/address/0x424242424b68330CcFb022881d25a6acC29852ea). If you cannot verify the route or token, stop before approving or sending GEN.
</Callout>

## Before you start

Bridging touches two chains, so you need both set up:

- **The GenLayer Chain (L2) in your wallet.** Add it from the [GenLayer Chain (L2)](/developers/networks#genlayer-chain-l2) section on the Networks page (it includes an "add to wallet" control). GEN is the **native** token on this chain — balances are denominated in wei (1 GEN = 10¹⁸ wei).
- **The corresponding L1 (Ethereum) network** in the same wallet, holding the GEN you want to deposit plus a little of the L1 gas token to pay for the deposit transaction.
- **Gas on both sides.** A deposit costs L1 gas; a withdrawal costs L2 gas to initiate and then L1 gas to finalize/claim on Ethereum.

<Callout type="info">
  Use the bridge for your specific GenLayer deployment. An Arbitrum One or Nova bridge address belongs to a different chain.
</Callout>

## Deposit: L1 → L2

Deposits are the fast direction. Bringing GEN from Ethereum onto the GenLayer Chain follows the standard rollup deposit flow:

1. **Connect your wallet** to the [Bradbury Phase 2 bridge](https://genlayer-chain-testnet-cfn8m3xc6l-86e44e69a1216817.testnets.rollbridge.app/) and select Ethereum Sepolia as the source. Use the wallet that holds your Sepolia GEN.
2. **Select the deposit direction** (L1 → GenLayer Chain) and the amount of GEN.
3. **Approve and deposit the GEN.** Check the spender and amount in the L1 token approval. Submit the native-token deposit through the chain’s ERC-20 Inbox.
4. **Verify the L2 credit.** When the sequencer includes the deposit, check the recipient’s native GEN balance. A native GEN deposit uses a direct Inbox credit. Contract calls and messages can use retryable tickets.

<Callout type="info">
  Once the deposit lands on L2, your GEN is the native currency of the GenLayer Chain — you can transfer it, pay fees with it, or [delegate it to a validator](/developers/delegating-gen).
</Callout>

## Withdraw: L2 → L1

A [canonical withdrawal](https://docs.arbitrum.io/how-arbitrum-works/deep-dives/l2-to-l1-messaging) requires a transaction on each chain.

1. **Initiate on L2.** Select the L1 recipient and submit the withdrawal through the bridge. The L2 transaction uses `ArbSys` to create an L2-to-L1 message. Save its transaction hash.
2. **Claim on L1.** Wait for the rollup assertion covering the message to be confirmed on Ethereum after the deployment’s challenge period. Once the message is executable, you or a relayer can submit its inclusion proof to the L1 Outbox. A successful execution releases ERC-20 GEN to the L1 recipient.

<Callout type="warning">
  **Check the message status before claiming.** The L2 receipt and publication of the batch are earlier milestones. The wait depends on the deployment’s challenge period and assertion confirmation.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info">
  The [GenLayer Finality Window](/understand-genlayer-protocol/core-concepts/optimistic-democracy/finality) governs appeals of Intelligent Contract results. The bridge uses the rollup assertion and the Outbox to determine when GEN can be claimed on Ethereum.
</Callout>

If the bridge offers a relayer, track its L1 execution transaction. Otherwise return to the bridge when the message becomes executable and submit the claim.

## Track a transfer

Because a transfer spans two chains, you follow it on both:

- **L2 side (GenLayer Chain).** Use the Chain Explorer listed on [Networks](/developers/networks). Confirm the deposit credit or locate the withdrawal transaction and its L2-to-L1 message.
- **L1 side (Ethereum).** For a deposit, check the L1 deposit receipt and then confirm the L2 credit. For a withdrawal, find the successful Outbox execution on the parent-chain explorer and verify the recipient’s ERC-20 GEN balance.
- **Bridge status page.** If the network's bridge UI shows a transfer/withdrawal status (e.g. "ready to finalize"), that is usually the clearest signal for when a withdrawal's L1 claim step becomes available.

<Callout type="info">
  Keep the L2 withdrawal hash so you can find the message and claim it when its assertion is confirmed.
</Callout>

## Common pitfalls

| Pitfall | What to do |
|---|---|
| Expecting a withdrawal to arrive as soon as the L2 receipt appears | Track the message until its assertion is confirmed and the Outbox claim succeeds. |
| Forgetting the L1 finalization step | A withdrawal is not complete until the L1 claim/finalize transaction succeeds. If your network's bridge doesn't auto-finalize, you must return and complete it. |
| No L1 gas to finalize | Keep some L1 gas token available to pay for the withdrawal's finalization transaction. |
| Using an address from another network | Match the route and Sepolia GEN token to the Bradbury links above. Verify the destination genesis hash on [Networks](/developers/networks#testnet-bradbury). |
| Confusing GenLayer finality with L2→L1 settlement | Follow the appeal window for an Intelligent Contract result and the Outbox message status for a bridge withdrawal. |
